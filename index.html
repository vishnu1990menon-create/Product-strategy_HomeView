Is HomeView the next Apple Watch?

On anchoring, it looks like a repeat.

Attached bets get a head start from the parent: customers, trust and time. The price is dependence on the parent's ecosystem.

Apple Watch and AirPods took that deal. Both need the iPhone for the full experience, and both are bought by iPhone owners. The Watch used the time to find its job in health.

Two other bets moved away from their parent's customer base.

Tesla's Powerwall never required a Tesla car. Its scale came through Megapack, sold to utilities.

Meta's glasses began in 2021 as an add-on to the VR and metaverse bet. Buyers now arrive through Ray-Ban and Oakley retail, and the pitch is an AI assistant, not the metaverse.

HomeView looks like the first group. Its household pitch is broad, but its personalised experience depends on paired Apple devices.

A watch serves a person. A home display serves a household.

In a mixed Apple–Android home, the buyer's ecosystem may not be everyone's. The anchor that gave the Watch time becomes a barrier at household scale.

Powerwall and Meta show the way out: stop requiring the parent's customers.

The test: can HomeView serve the whole household, not just the Apple users in it?

2. Feature growth by generation

Radar charts count cumulative additions per area (tagged from the version tables) and overlay selected generations, so the shape shows where the product's weight moved. Apple Watch: from communication to clinical health

  • Communication, the launch pitch, grows modestly (2 additions at launch, 5 today).
  • Clinical health sensing goes from none in 2015 to the largest area (9 additions): ECG, fall detection, blood oxygen, temperature, crash detection, sleep apnea, hypertension, sleep score, upgraded heart-rate sensing.
  • Standalone connectivity arrives in steps: cellular (2017), emergency calling (2019), 5G and satellite (2025).
  • The radar rotates from a communication-led watch to a health-led one. That is the pivot the iPhone anchor paid for, and every feature still runs through the iPhone relationship.

AirPods: one job, widened

  • Launch was convenience (pairing, case) plus Siri. That area keeps growing (8 additions) but the shape stays recognisably the same.
  • Noise control and audio immersion become the largest area (9 additions) from the 2019 Pro onward.
  • Health and hearing (2) and intelligence (3) are layers added later, not a change of job.
  • This is the "match" case: the radar grows outward without rotating, inside the iPhone ecosystem.

Tesla Powerwall: capacity first, then power, solar and grid

  • Capacity doubles in 2017 (6.4 to 13.5 kWh) and then stays flat.
  • Powerwall 3 raises continuous power from 5 to 11.5 kW, integrates the solar inverter and takes a grid role (virtual power plants from 2022).
  • None of these features depends on owning a Tesla car, which is why the feature set could grow toward the grid and utilities.
  • Capacity and power are normalized to Powerwall 3. Solar integration and grid participation are analyst-scored (0, 25, 50, 100%).

Meta glasses: capture and audio first, then AI, then a display

  • The 2021 product was a camera and speaker pair tied to a Facebook account, pitched as a metaverse add-on.

Powerwall and Meta broke the demand link. Powerwall was never bought because someone owned a Tesla; Meta's buyers arrive through Ray-Ban and Oakley retail. Watch and AirPods keep every link to the iPhone, and HomeView, as described, would too.

Meta keeps a technical link (Meta account and Meta AI app) and its parent funds Reality Labs losses, so it detached on demand, not on everything. It also has no phone OS, so it relies on an iOS or Android companion app it does not control. That is an analytical judgment, not a sourced finding.

The anchor was not a successful core product but a struggling platform, so the glasses outgrew it instead of riding it.

Strong anchor: Watch, AirPods, HomeView

  • Buyers, distribution and trust come with the parent's installed base.
  • Time to find the job: the Watch needed three to four years to settle on health.
  • Reach stops where the parent ecosystem stops.

Loose or migrating anchor: Powerwall, Meta glasses

  • Shared brand, cells and capital (Powerwall), or a funded platform (Meta), without requiring the parent's customers.
  • Slower early adoption for Powerwall: about five years to 100,000 installs.
  • Value moved to a bigger market: Megapack for Powerwall, AI glasses for Meta.

5. HomeView: the same anchor, a bigger problem

HomeView is not covered by the source data. This section applies the anchoring pattern to the premise in the post; it is a framing, not a measurement.

A watch serves a person

One wearer, one phone, one ecosystem. The iPhone anchor limits reach, but the person who buys it is the person who uses it.

Paired iPhoneHealth dataPersonal alerts

A home display serves a household

Several people, possibly several ecosystems. The buyer's ecosystem may not be everyone's, so the anchor limits the people who use it, not just the people who buy it.

Apple user: full experienceAndroid user: limitedGuests, kids, partners

HomeView is set up to repeat the Watch and AirPods pattern.

It matches them on every link in section 3: Apple-owner buyers and an experience that needs paired Apple devices. The Watch and AirPods can live with that because one person owns both the phone and the product. A household product multiplies the problem, because each non-Apple member sees a limited version.

Powerwall and Meta show the way out: stop requiring the parent's customers. For HomeView, the test is whether it can serve the whole household, not just the Apple users within it.
